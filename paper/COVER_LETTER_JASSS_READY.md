# Cover Letter - Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation

**Manuscript title:** *Can a Synthetic Collective Rival an Elite Team? A Reproducible Social Simulation of Composition, Variability, and Context Dependence*

Dear Editors,

Please consider my manuscript, *“Can a Synthetic Collective Rival an Elite Team? A Reproducible Social Simulation of Composition, Variability, and Context Dependence,”* for publication as a research article in the *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*.

The manuscript introduces the **synthetic collective** as a simulated group whose members do not correspond one-to-one to existing persons but instantiate role-specific profiles derived from an empirical population. I use a rule-bounded, highly interdependent team environment to compare two composition logics: a Synthetic XI constructed from robust positional archetypes and a Real Best XI composed of distinct elite players.

The paper is not intended as a sports forecast or as a claim that artificial intelligence defeats or replaces human athletes. Its central social-simulation question is how distributional and elite composition produce different collective-performance signatures. The study examines championship probability, variability, lower-tail risk, extreme outcomes, and state dependence under a common event-state model.

The study was preregistered and uses a fail-closed release architecture. The primary experiment contains 10,000 simulated elimination finals. An independently seeded, preregistered 50,000-run precision replication reproduces the primary estimate within 0.106 percentage points. The two collectives nevertheless differ in the shape of their performance distributions: the synthetic collective is less variable in major outputs but has greater zero-output and lower-tail offensive risk, whereas the elite collective generates high-impact outcomes more frequently. The relative advantage also changes with score state, match phase, and numerical state.

A further contribution is epistemological. The narrow Monte Carlo intervals and near-identical independent replication demonstrate high computational precision under the frozen specification. Nine sensitivity scenarios and a nested analysis across 160 parameter worlds show substantially wider structural uncertainty. The paper therefore demonstrates that Monte Carlo reproducibility and model-specification robustness are different properties.

I believe the manuscript is well suited to JASSS because it:

- uses computer simulation to investigate a social and collective process;
- develops a conceptual object relevant to artificial societies and collective intelligence;
- provides an ODD-aligned model description and a complete ODD supplement;
- separates verification, validation, confirmation, replication, sensitivity, and structural uncertainty;
- provides frozen code, data, seeds, manifests, source hashes, and a claim-to-artifact provenance matrix.

The manuscript is anonymized for double-blind review. A separate title page contains the author's identity and affiliation. An identity-scrubbed reproducibility package accompanies the submission; it includes the frozen code, critical inputs, preregistration, tests, seed ledgers, results, manifests, ODD documentation, and reproduction instructions. An independent clean-room audit regenerated the 10,000-run confirmatory experiment and reproduced all 15 artifacts in the frozen manifest byte for byte.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. I approve the submission and accept responsibility for the work. Generative AI systems are not authors; any editorial or software-development assistance is disclosed, and I retain responsibility for every claim, reference, analysis, and line of submitted code.
