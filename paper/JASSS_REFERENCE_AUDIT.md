# JASSS Reference Audit and Literature Positioning

## Purpose

This document controls the theoretical and methodological bibliography for the JASSS submission. It distinguishes references already integrated into the manuscript from references recommended for the final literature revision. Every DOI should be checked again during final typesetting.

## A. Core collective-intelligence literature

### Already integrated

1. **Woolley, A. W., Chabris, C. F., Pentland, A., Hashmi, N., and Malone, T. W. (2010).** Evidence for a collective intelligence factor in the performance of human groups. *Science*, 330(6004), 686–688. DOI: `10.1126/science.1193147`.
   - Use: establishes collective performance as a group-level property not reducible to individual ability.

2. **Hong, L., and Page, S. E. (2004).** Groups of diverse problem solvers can outperform groups of high-ability problem solvers. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 101(46), 16385–16389. DOI: `10.1073/pnas.0403723101`.
   - Use: formal basis for composition and complementarity versus simple individual optimization.

3. **Swaab, R. I., Schaerer, M., Anicich, E. M., Ronay, R., and Galinsky, A. D. (2014).** The too-much-talent effect: Team interdependence determines when more talent is too much or not enough. *Psychological Science*, 25(8), 1581–1591. DOI: `10.1177/0956797614537280`.
   - Use: cautions against assuming monotonic returns from elite talent under high interdependence.

### Add in final revision

4. **Woolley, A. W., Aggarwal, I., and Malone, T. W. (2015).** Collective intelligence and group performance. *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, 24(6), 420–424. DOI: `10.1177/0963721415599543`.
   - Use: concise framework separating group composition from group interaction.

5. **Woolley, A. W., Chow, R. M., Mayo, A. T., Riedl, C., and Chang, J. W. (2023).** Collective attention and collective intelligence: The role of hierarchy and team gender composition. *Organization Science*, 34(3), 1315–1331. DOI: `10.1287/orsc.2022.1602`.
   - Use: strengthens the link between attention allocation, composition, and collective intelligence.

6. **Glover, J., and Kim, E. (2021).** Optimal team composition: Diversity to foster implicit team incentives. *Management Science*, 67(9), 5800–5820. DOI: `10.1287/mnsc.2020.3762`.
   - Use: formal team-design perspective on specialization, heterogeneity, complementarity, and cooperation.

## B. Artificial and synthetic collectives

### Already integrated

7. **Casadei, R. (2023).** Artificial collective intelligence engineering: A survey of concepts and perspectives. *Artificial Life*, 29(4), 433–467. DOI: `10.1162/artl_a_00408`.
   - Use: places the paper within engineered collective intelligence.

8. **Cui, H., and Yasseri, T. (2024).** AI-enhanced collective intelligence. *Patterns*, 5(11), 101074. DOI: `10.1016/j.patter.2024.101074`.
   - Use: connects the contribution to emerging AI-mediated collective intelligence while clarifying that the Synthetic XI is not a human–AI team.

9. **Seeber, I., et al. (2020).** Machines as teammates: A research agenda on AI in team collaboration. *Information & Management*, 57(2), 103174. DOI: `10.1016/j.im.2019.103174`.
   - Use: establishes the neighboring human–AI team literature.

10. **Ashery, A. F., Aiello, L. M., and Baronchelli, A. (2025).** Emergent social conventions and collective bias in LLM populations. *Science Advances*, 11(20), eadu9368. DOI: `10.1126/sciadv.adu9368`.
    - Use: contrasts distributionally constructed agents with communicative LLM populations.

11. **Park, J. S., O'Brien, J. C., Cai, C. J., Morris, M. R., Liang, P., and Bernstein, M. S. (2023).** Generative agents: Interactive simulacra of human behavior. *Proceedings of UIST 2023*. DOI: `10.1145/3586183.3606763`.
    - Use: positions the study relative to socially interactive generative agents.

### Add in final revision

12. **Solé, R., Amor, D. R., Duran-Nebreda, S., Conde-Pueyo, N., Carbonell-Ballestero, M., and Montañez, R. (2016).** Synthetic collective intelligence. *BioSystems*, 148, 47–61. DOI: `10.1016/j.biosystems.2016.01.002`.
    - Use: the closest terminological precedent. The manuscript must explicitly distinguish this paper's distributionally constructed social-simulation collective from biological and embodied synthetic collective intelligence.

13. **Kaur, H., and Kahlon, K. S. (2015).** Dynamics of artificial agent societies: A survey and an agent migration perspective. *AI Communications*, 28(3). DOI: `10.3233/AIC-150664`.
    - Use: broad artificial-agent-society context; optional if space permits.

## C. Synthetic populations and empirical agent construction

### Already integrated

14. **Chapuis, K., Taillandier, P., and Drogoul, A. (2022).** Generation of synthetic populations in social simulations: A review of methods and practices. *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*, 25(2), 6. DOI: `10.18564/jasss.4762`.
    - Use: distinguishes population reconstruction from the construction of an interacting synthetic collective.

### Recommended addition

15. **Laatabi, A., Marilleau, N., Nguyen-Huu, T., Hbid, H., and Ait Babram, M. (2018).** ODD+2D: An ODD based protocol for mapping data to empirical ABMs. *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*, 21(2), 9. DOI: `10.18564/jasss.3646`.
    - Use: supports explicit mapping between empirical data, profile construction, and agents.

## D. Team performance and football as an interdependent system

### Already integrated

16. **Grund, T. U. (2012).** Network structure and team performance: The case of English Premier League soccer teams. *Social Networks*, 34(4), 682–690. DOI: `10.1016/j.socnet.2012.08.004`.
    - Use: supports the importance of interaction structure in football.

17. **Franck, E., and Nüesch, S. (2010).** The effect of talent disparity on team productivity in soccer. *Journal of Economic Psychology*, 31(2), 218–229. DOI: `10.1016/j.joep.2009.12.003`.
    - Use: links talent distribution and complementarity in football teams.

### Possible addition

18. **Higgs, M., Plewnia, U., and Ploch, J. (2005).** Influence of team composition and task complexity on team performance. *Team Performance Management*, 11. DOI: `10.1108/13527590510635134`.
    - Use: supports the claim that the value of diversity depends on task complexity. Verify issue and page details before use.

## E. ODD, transparency, and model communication

### Already integrated

19. **Grimm, V., et al. (2020).** The ODD protocol for describing agent-based and other simulation models: A second update to improve clarity, replication, and structural realism. *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*, 23(2), 7. DOI: `10.18564/jasss.4259`.
    - Use: governs main-text ODD summary plus full supplementary ODD.

20. **Cioffi-Revilla, C. (2010).** A methodology for complex social simulations. *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*, 13(1), 7.
    - Use: positions the model as a staged research program rather than a one-shot simulation.

### Add in final revision

21. **Polhill, J. G., Parker, D., Brown, D., and Grimm, V. (2008).** Using the ODD protocol for describing three agent-based social simulation models of land-use change. *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*, 11(2), 3.
    - Use: supports ODD's role in communication, comparison, and replication of social simulations.

## F. Verification, validation, replication, and uncertainty

### Already integrated

22. **Windrum, P., Fagiolo, G., and Moneta, A. (2007).** Empirical validation of agent-based models: Alternatives and prospects. *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*, 10(2), 8.
    - Use: provides the foundational validation frame.

### Add in final revision

23. **Collins, A., Koehler, M., and Lynch, C. (2024).** Methods that support the validation of agent-based models: An overview and discussion. *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*, 27(1), 11. DOI: `10.18564/jasss.5258`.
    - Use: current validation vocabulary and fitness-for-purpose framing.

24. **McCulloch, J., Ge, J., Ward, J. A., Heppenstall, A., Polhill, J. G., and Malleson, N. (2022).** Calibrating agent-based models using uncertainty quantification methods. *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*, 25(2), 1. DOI: `10.18564/jasss.4791`.
    - Use: supports the separation and quantification of parameter uncertainty.

25. **Lee, J.-S., Filatova, T., Ligmann-Zielinska, A., Hassani-Mahmooei, B., Stonedahl, F., Lorscheid, I., Voinov, A., Polhill, J. G., Sun, Z., and Parker, D. C. (2015).** The complexities of agent-based modeling output analysis. *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*, 18(4), 4. DOI: `10.18564/jasss.2897`.
    - Use: supports multidimensional output analysis and sensitivity rather than reliance on one mean result.

26. **Hauke, J., Achter, S., and Meyer, M. (2020).** Theory development via replicated simulations and the added value of standards. *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*, 23(1), 12. DOI: `10.18564/jasss.4219`.
    - Use: directly supports independent simulation replication as a path to stronger theory.

## G. Final literature revision tasks

Before submission:

1. integrate Solé et al. (2016) explicitly because it is the closest title-level precedent;
2. add Woolley, Aggarwal, and Malone (2015) to strengthen the composition/interaction distinction;
3. add Collins et al. (2024), McCulloch et al. (2022), Lee et al. (2015), and Hauke et al. (2020) to the methodological section;
4. add ODD+2D where the data-to-agent mapping is described;
5. verify every author list, volume, issue, article number, page range, and DOI through the publisher or Crossref;
6. avoid citation padding: each reference must perform a distinct argumentative function;
7. inspect the previous three years of JASSS for papers on artificial societies, collective behavior, empirical ABMs, calibration, and model validation, and cite only directly relevant work;
8. maintain a balanced reference base beyond JASSS so the paper does not appear journal-engineered rather than field-grounded.
