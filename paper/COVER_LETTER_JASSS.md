# Cover Letter — Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation

**Manuscript title:** *Can a Synthetic Collective Rival an Elite Team? A Reproducible Social Simulation of Composition, Variability, and Context Dependence*

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, *“Can a Synthetic Collective Rival an Elite Team? A Reproducible Social Simulation of Composition, Variability, and Context Dependence,”* for publication as a research article in the *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation*.

The manuscript introduces the **synthetic collective** as a simulated group whose members do not correspond one-to-one to existing persons but instantiate role-specific profiles derived from an empirical population. We use a rule-bounded, highly interdependent team environment to compare two composition logics: a Synthetic XI constructed from robust positional archetypes and a Real Best XI composed of distinct elite players.

The paper is not intended as a sports forecast or as a claim that artificial intelligence defeats or replaces human athletes. Its central social-simulation question is how distributional and elite composition produce different collective-performance signatures. We examine championship probability, variability, lower-tail risk, extreme outcomes, and state dependence under a common event-state model.

The study was preregistered and uses a fail-closed release architecture. The primary experiment contains 10,000 simulated elimination finals. An independently seeded, preregistered 50,000-run precision replication reproduces the primary estimate within 0.106 percentage points. The two collectives nevertheless differ in the shape of their performance distributions: the synthetic collective is less variable in major outputs but has greater zero-output and lower-tail offensive risk, whereas the elite collective generates high-impact outcomes more frequently. The relative advantage also changes with score state, match phase, and numerical state.

A further contribution is epistemological. The narrow Monte Carlo intervals and near-identical independent replication demonstrate high computational precision under the frozen specification. Nine sensitivity scenarios and a nested analysis across 160 parameter worlds show substantially wider structural uncertainty. The paper therefore offers an explicit demonstration that Monte Carlo reproducibility and model-specification robustness are different properties.
